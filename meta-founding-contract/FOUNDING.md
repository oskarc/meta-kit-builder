## The statement

*Given by the Pioneer, 2026-09-13. Recorded verbatim.*

> The long-term goal of this approach is to build kits that travel: kits that reproduce the same standard of development wherever they are used, so that an API built with one adheres to the organisation's standards for patterns, logging, error handling and the rest — and so that a non-developer can build with it.
>
> An organisation's standards are rarely written down or top of mind, but they are always known. This is where Peter Naur's theory building comes in: the standard lives as the developer's theory of the work, and the kit's job is to draw it out. The standard is what gets built, progressively, by the developer building the kit — the skills that make up the kit *are* the organisation's standards.
>
> The kit approach is therefore more than the goal above. It is a way of maturing the end result and guiding the developer there. Developers may believe they have never worked with standards; what they know and feel about good work are standards, whether or not they have been named.
>
> We call the human the pioneer because the end result is not known to them at the start. The journey is characterised by exploration, and by collaboration between human and agent.
>
> So the practice guides the pioneer, while the pioneer guides and directs the kits being developed.
